# Australia Trip prep: fact-check

Texts for the AU trip and its six region packs were checked against English Wikipedia (one article
per species, or per genus where an article covers several). eBird, Birds of the World, BirdLife
Australia and the Australian Museum were mostly unreachable or stub-only. All new lines are in our
own words; no source text is quoted.

## Summary

- Species `look_for` (548 species new to the AU work): about 330 species changed, EN and NL together.
- Groups (22 new group files) and `au/texts.json`: 20 group files and about 24 places in the country
  texts changed.
- Pack `texts.json` (wettropics, topend, redcentre, tasmania, southeast): 19 corrections; southwest none.
- Species that already existed before the AU work (shared with NZ, Costa Rica etc.) were not checked.

## What could not be verified

Many honeyeater, kingfisher, scrubwren and thornbill articles are stubs without plumage detail, so
about 200 individual claims were left as they were. Each report below lists them. Notable doubts:

- Chiming Wedgebill "inland from Perth" and Red-lored Whistler "black breast band" (au-southwest):
  no source found, left unchanged.
- Collared Imperial-Pigeon is in Australia only on the Torres Strait islands; its place in the
  au-topend list is questionable.
- Lovely Fairywren and Zebra Finch: the fetched pages were unclear, so the lines are best effort.
- Spotted Pardalote: Wikipedia places the white spots on wings and tail, field guides show a spotted
  crown; line left as it was.
- AU `here` texts for waterfowl, seabirds, raptors and parrots could not be fetched and stay unchecked.

Per-agent reports follow (old and new English lines, reasons, unverified lists).
