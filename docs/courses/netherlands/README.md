# Birds of the Netherlands in a year

A course that teaches a beginner the birds of one country, one lesson a week for a year. This
folder holds the plan, the first lessons as content files, and notes on the photos they use.

- `lessons/lesson-NN-*.json`: lesson content in English and Dutch, with the chosen media ids.
- `media-flags.csv`: photos found while picking that should not be quiz material.

## Who it is for, and the idea

Motmot's quizzes test what you already know. A beginner needs something that **teaches**:
a few birds at a time, the one feature to look at, and then practice until it sticks.

Four principles shape every lesson:

1. **Familiar before abundant.** Commonness data puts White-fronted Goose and Black-headed Gull at
   the top for the Netherlands, but a beginner meets a Blackbird first. The early lessons order
   birds by how likely you are to see them *where you live*: high occupancy, near houses and water.
   Abundance ranking takes over once the basics are in.
2. **One version of a bird at a time.** Lesson 1 teaches the *male* Blackbird and the *male* House
   Sparrow, the adult Mute Swan, the adult gull. Females, young birds and winter plumage get their
   own lessons later ("the other half", week 5; ageing gulls, week 27). Quiz photos only show the
   version that was taught.
3. **Words when you need them.** Every lesson adds three to five words for parts of a bird (crown,
   bib, frontal shield, nape...), used in that lesson's "look for" lines. By the time the deep dives
   start, the learner already speaks the language of tertials and primary projection.
4. **Outside, not just on screen.** Each lesson ends with a small task for the coming week ("Is one
   of your blackbirds brown?"). Learning birds happens outside; the app gives you something to look for.

## Course structure

52 weeks in four parts. Most lessons work in any season; a few are tied to it (marked ☀ spring/summer,
❄ winter, 🍂 autumn). When a learner reaches a seasonal lesson out of season, the app offers to swap
it with the next lesson that fits, so geese are taught when geese are here.

## Lesson format

Each lesson takes ten to fifteen minutes and always runs the same way: an introduction, then the
birds one by one, and the quiz only at the very end. The `flow` field in each lesson file lists the
screens in order.

1. **Introduction.** Where we are and what we will look at: "We nemen een kijkje in de
   achtertuin." Two or three short paragraphs that set the scene, say how many birds the lesson
   has, and say what comes at the end. Thumbnails of the birds you are about to meet.
2. **The birds, one screen each.** A hand-picked photo of the taught version (labelled "adult
   male"), the name in the app language with the scientific name, three "look for" lines, where and
   when, a play button for the sound, and sometimes a note pointing to a later lesson. Words for
   parts of a bird are in bold, with their meaning right under the look-for lines; there is no
   separate vocabulary step.
3. **Spot the difference** sits on the card of the second bird of a confusing pair (Blue Tit after
   Great Tit, Moorhen after Coot): a small side-by-side with the bird you just saw.
4. **Quiz, at the end.** Eight to ten photo questions. Answers come from this lesson's birds; from
   week 2 onwards two or three questions repeat birds from earlier lessons. Mistakes go to *My
   tricky birds*. Nothing is tested before this point.
5. **Done.** Score, the task for this week outside with a checklist of the lesson's birds to tick
   off when seen, and when the next lesson opens.

A new lesson opens every week (Monday morning, with a push notification). Earlier lessons stay
open for review, and a learner who is behind can do two in one week. Nobody can run ahead more
than one lesson, which keeps the course a habit rather than a binge.

## Media

### Photos

The `media` block of each bird lists two groups of media ids:

- `card`: the photo on the bird card. Sharp, the whole bird, the taught plumage, a typical pose.
- `quiz`: photos for practice questions. Still the taught plumage, but more varied: other angles,
  further away, in a typical setting.

`media_for_later` keeps photos of other plumages that came up while picking (female Blackbirds,
cygnets, juvenile Moorhens) for the lessons that need them.

The database has no sex, age or plumage on media, so a random photo of "Blackbird" can be a female,
a juvenile or a leucistic bird. For the first lessons the photos are picked by hand. For a whole
year that will not scale; the plan is:

1. Add `sex` (male, female, unknown), `age` (adult, juvenile, immature, first-winter...) and
   `plumage` (breeding, non-breeding, eclipse) to media.
2. Fill them with a vision model in a batch job per species, starting with the course species.
3. Let reviewers confirm or correct them in the existing media review screen.

Lessons can then ask for "adult male Blackbird" instead of listing ids.

### Sounds

The local media dump has no audio, so sounds are not picked yet. Each bird has a `sound` block
saying what to pick (`song` or `call`, region, quality), for example *"xeno-canto song, NL/BE/DE,
quality A, under 30 s, no other birds"*. Choosing them needs the production media table, where the
xeno-canto recordings are.

### What picking the photos turned up

Picking photos for 16 species meant looking at about 400. `media-flags.csv` lists 59 that should not
be shown in a quiz, with the reason:

- **Not a bird:** single feathers (5 of the 24 Wood Pigeon photos looked at), a leg ring, a sign,
  photos where no bird is visible.
- **Possibly the wrong species:** white birds and gulls among the Feral Pigeons, egrets among the
  Grey Herons, an orange dove among the Collared Doves, Egyptian Geese among the Mallards.
- **Misleading for beginners:** leucistic Blackbirds and Jackdaws, farmyard Mallards.

Feral Pigeon was the worst: 8 of 24 photos were unusable. The flags are worth going through in
media review regardless of the course, since these photos show up in normal quizzes too.

## Data notes

- **Species ids** in the lesson files were matched through the Wikimedia file names in the local
  media dump, checked by eye against the photos, and confirmed against motmot.club: all 16 are
  right. Names follow the database (Coloeus monedula for the Jackdaw, Rock Dove / rotsduif for the
  Feral Pigeon, which the lesson calls stadsduif and explains).
- **Sounds:** every lesson bird has 17-20 xeno-canto recordings in the database (`sound.available`),
  but the API gives no song/call type, country or quality. Picking per the `sound.pick` spec needs
  the xeno-canto metadata.
- **The Netherlands commonness list includes Caribbean species.** `jizz/data/commonness_NL.csv`
  ranks Antillean Crested Hummingbird at #80, with American Flamingo, Laughing Gull and Audubon's
  Shearwater also on it: the eBird data for "NL" includes the Caribbean Netherlands. The course
  ignores them, but they likely show up in the Netherlands quizzes and country pages too.
- **Feral Pigeon is under-counted.** eBird observers skip feral pigeons, so `rocpig` ranks #127 in
  the Netherlands while it is one of the most-seen birds in any town. Familiarity has to come from
  the course author, not only from the data.

## What it would take to build

1. **Content model.** `Course` (country, title, languages), `Lesson` (course, week, kind, season,
   texts), `LessonBird` (lesson, species, plumage label, look-for lines, sound, card and quiz
   media), `LessonProgress` (player, lesson, started, completed, score). Texts as per-language JSON
   like the files here, so a course can be written once and translated.
2. **Import.** A management command that loads `docs/courses/<country>/lessons/*.json`.
3. **App screens.** Course overview (the year as a path), lesson flow (introduction, one screen
   per bird, quiz, done), and a home-screen card for "this week's lesson". The quiz at the end
   reuses the quiz engine with a fixed species list and media list.
4. **Weekly rhythm.** Unlock on Monday, push notification, streak.
5. **Media tags** as above, so later lessons do not need hand-picked ids.

Week 1 to 10 can ship with hand-picked media; the media tags are needed before the deep dives.
